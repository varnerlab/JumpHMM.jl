Sector Factor Model

The MySectorFactorModel type holds a model of many assets, built from three layers of hidden Markov models: one model of the market, one model of each sector, and one own model of each asset. On day $t$, the growth rate of asset $i$ in sector $s$ is

\[g_i(t) = \alpha_i + \beta_i(t)\, g_m(t) + \delta_i\, f_s(t) + \psi_i\, \varepsilon_i(t)\]

where $g_m(t)$ is a draw of the market model, $f_s(t)$ is a draw of the model of sector $s$, and $\varepsilon_i(t)$ is a draw of the own model of asset $i$, all in 1/year. The own draw is not centered, because the own model is fit to a residual that averages zero in the data. The intercept $\alpha_i$ (1/year), the market sensitivity $\beta_i(t)$, the sector loading $\delta_i$, and the own scale $\psi_i$ come from the fit below.

The assets are linked only through the market draw and the sector draw that they have in common on each day. A copula would reorder the draws of each asset across days, which would break up the volatility clustering and the timing of the jump episodes that each model produces.

What each term is fit to

The build function fits each term to its part of the data:

  • Market term: We regress the growth rate of each asset on the growth rate of the market by ordinary least squares, $g_i(t) = \hat{\alpha}_i + \beta_i\, g_m(t) + e_i(t)$. The fit gives the intercept $\hat{\alpha}_i$, the sensitivity $\beta_i$, the R² of each asset (r²), and the residual $e_i(t)$.
  • Sector factor: The factor $f_s(t)$ of sector $s$ is the mean residual of the assets in that sector on day $t$ (sector_factors).
  • Sector loading: The loading $\delta_i$ starts as the slope of the residual of asset $i$ on the mean residual of the other assets in its sector (loo_loadings). We then scale the loadings of each sector by one number, so that the mean of $\delta_i \delta_j \operatorname{Var}(f_s)$ over the pairs of assets in the sector equals the mean covariance of their residuals (sector_loadings).
  • Own term: The own model of asset $i$ is fit to the residual left after the sector term, $u_i(t) = e_i(t) - \delta_i f_s(t)$ (own_residuals).
  • Hidden Markov models: The market model is fit to the growth rates of the market. The sector and own models are fit to the growth rates of level series that compound $f_s$ and $u_i$ from a start of 100.0 (sector_levels, own_levels). These growth rates equal $f_s$ and $u_i$ up to rounding.

Every sector needs at least two assets, because the loading of an asset uses the other assets in its sector.

Intercepts and the drift of β

By default, the intercept $\alpha_i$ is the fitted $\hat{\alpha}_i$, and $\beta_i(t)$ stays at the fitted $\beta_i$. The optional keys intercept_pull, β_scale, and β_halflife change these defaults.

The key intercept_pull moves each intercept toward the mean intercept $\bar{\alpha}$ of all the assets, $\alpha_i = \bar{\alpha} + c\,(\hat{\alpha}_i - \bar{\alpha})$, where $c$ is intercept_pull. A value of 1.0, the default, keeps the fitted intercepts, and 0.0 gives every asset the mean intercept.

The keys β_scale, with one value per asset, and β_halflife make the sensitivity drift, $\beta_i(t) = \beta_i + x_i(t)$. The drift $x_i$ is an exponential moving average of standard normal shocks $z_i(t)$ (drift_path):

\[x_i(t) = (1 - a)\, x_i(t-1) + a\, k_i\, z_i(t), \qquad a = 1 - 2^{-1/h}\]

where $k_i$ is the drift scale of asset $i$ (β_scale[i]) and $h$ is the half-life of the drift, in trading days (β_halflife, default 126.0). The first value is drawn from the stationary distribution of the drift. The drift then has the same variance $\sigma_{x,i}^{2} = k_i^{2}\, a/(2 - a)$ on every day (drift_variance).

The default β_scale is all zeros, which keeps every $\beta_i$ fixed.

The own scale

The own scale $\psi_i$ makes the drift of $\beta_i$, the sector term, and the own term together carry the variance of the residual $e_i$ (own_variances, own_scale):

\[\psi_i^{2} = \frac{\operatorname{Var}(e_i) - \delta_i^{2} \operatorname{Var}(f_s) - \sigma_{x,i}^{2} \operatorname{Var}(g_m)}{\operatorname{Var}(u_i)}\]

The residual $e_i$ comes from a fit with a fixed $\beta_i$. That residual already holds the movement of the stock's actual $\beta$ over time. We subtract $\sigma_{x,i}^{2} \operatorname{Var}(g_m)$, so that a drifting $\beta_i$ does not add that variance a second time.

The sector factor averages the residuals of its assets, asset $i$ included. As a result, part of $u_i$ still moves with $f_s$. Independent draws of the sector model and the own model lose that shared part, and $\psi_i$ puts that variance back.

Usage

The tickers list includes the market, and sector has one label for each asset, without the market:

import JumpHMM

tickers = ["SPY", "JPM", "BAC", "XOM", "CVX"];             # one ticker for each column of prices
sector = ["Financials", "Financials", "Energy", "Energy"];  # one label for each asset, without the market

model = JumpHMM.build(JumpHMM.MySectorFactorModel, (
    tickers = tickers,
    prices = prices,          # the share prices (USD/share), one row per day and one column per ticker
    market = "SPY",           # the ticker of the market
    sector = sector,
    number_of_states = 100,
    ν = 5.0,
    β_scale = fill(0.3, 4),   # optional: the drift scale of β for each asset (default all 0.0)
    β_halflife = 126.0        # optional: the half-life of the drift, in trading days (default 126.0)
));
model.tickers                 # ["JPM", "BAC", "XOM", "CVX"]: the assets, without the market

path = JumpHMM.sample_dependence(model, 252; seed = 5660);
path.G                        # the growth rates of the assets (1/year), one row per day and one column per asset

The keys market_model, sector_models, and own_models pass in models fitted or tuned elsewhere, instead of the models the build would fit. Each own model must describe the residual of its asset after the sector term, the series that own_levels holds. The build docstring lists every optional key and its default.

Draws and composition

A path of the universe comes from two steps:

  • Draw: The sample_draws function draws every random part of one path. These parts are one market path, one path of each sector model, one path of each own model, and the standard normal shocks of the drift of $\beta$.
  • Compose: The compose function combines the draws with the parameters of the model into the growth rates of the assets.

The sample_dependence function does both steps. The table lists the keys of each result:

KeyWhat it holdsReturned by
g_mThe market growth rates (1/year), one per daysample_draws, sample_dependence
m_statesThe state of the market model on each daysample_draws, sample_dependence
episodesA flag that is true on the days of a market jump episodesample_draws, sample_dependence
FThe sector factors (1/year), one column per sectorsample_draws, sample_dependence
sector_episodesA flag that is true on the days of a jump episode of each sector modelsample_draws
EThe own draws (1/year), one column per assetsample_draws
own_episodesA flag that is true on the days of a jump episode of each own modelsample_draws
ZThe standard normal shocks of the drift of $\beta$, one column per assetsample_draws
GThe growth rates of the assets (1/year), one column per assetcompose, sample_dependence
β_pathThe market sensitivity of each asset on each daycompose, sample_dependence
name_episodesA flag that is true on the days when the market, the sector, or the own draw of an asset is in a jump episodecompose, sample_dependence

Each part of the draw has its own seed, made from seed. The same seed gives the same path, and seed must be an Int64 from 0 up to, but not including, 10^8. Like simulate, the function sample_draws reseeds the global random number generator.

Composing the same draws under new parameters

We keep the draws, so that we can compose them again under new parameters. The with_parameters function makes a new model with new intercepts, a new half-life, or new drift scales, and it recomputes $\psi$. The new model shares the fitted hidden Markov models with the input model.

# draw 20 paths once, and keep the draws -
number_of_days = 252;
bank = Array{NamedTuple,1}();
for p ∈ 1:20
    push!(bank, JumpHMM.sample_draws(model, number_of_days; seed = p));
end

# compose the same draws under three drift scales -
for k ∈ [0.1, 0.3, 0.5]
    trial = JumpHMM.with_parameters(model; β_scale = fill(k, length(model.tickers)));
    for p ∈ 1:20
        G = JumpHMM.compose(trial, bank[p]).G;   # the growth rates (1/year), days × assets
        # compare a statistic of G with its value in the data -
    end
end

The compose_name function composes one asset alone, with its own intercept, half-life, or drift scale for that call. Because compose reads only the keys it needs, the draws can mix the parts of two draws, for example the market from one draw and the sectors from another.

Changing a fitted model

The build computes the own scale $\psi_i$ (ψ) from the variances of the data and the drift of $\beta$. Change the intercepts and the drift of $\beta$ with with_parameters, which recomputes $\psi$.

The functions compose and compose_name compute the own scale again from the variances. A value written into ψ by hand therefore has no effect.

The own scale does not depend on the fitted hidden Markov models. After you change the jump settings or the emissions of the market model, a sector model, or an own model, you can compose with the changed model directly. To get a separate model with the changed models, call the build again with the keys market_model, sector_models, and own_models. The rebuild must repeat the settings of the first build, because every key it leaves out takes its default:

own_models = deepcopy(model.own_models);
own_models[1].ϵ = 2e-3;          # the probability that a jump episode starts on a given day, for the first asset
rebuilt = JumpHMM.build(JumpHMM.MySectorFactorModel, (
    tickers = tickers, prices = prices, market = "SPY", sector = sector, number_of_states = 100, ν = 5.0,
    β_scale = model.β_scale,              # the settings of the first build
    β_halflife = model.β_halflife,
    market_model = model.market_model, sector_models = model.sector_models, own_models = own_models
));

The model from with_parameters shares its fitted models with the input model. A change to one of those fitted models changes both sector factor models. Make a deepcopy of the model before a study that edits its fitted models.

Tuning, simulating, and validating

To tune the market, sector, and own models, build a portfolio with dependence = JumpHMM.MySectorFactorModel. The portfolio also simulates the universe, and it validates each own model against the residual series it was fit to. The portfolio takes the same sector key, and it takes the other keys of this build in sector_options.
