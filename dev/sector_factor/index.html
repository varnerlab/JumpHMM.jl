Sector Factor Model

Overview

The sector factor model builds a synthetic universe from three layers of Jump-HMMs: one for the market, one for each sector, and one for each name. The daily growth rate of name $i$ in sector $s$ is

\[g_i(t) = \alpha_i + \beta_i(t)\, g_m(t) + \delta_i\, f_s(t) + s_i\, \tilde{\varepsilon}_i(t)\]

where $g_m(t)$ is one draw of the market model, $f_s(t)$ one draw of the model of the name's sector, and $\tilde{\varepsilon}_i(t)$ one draw of the name's own model, centered on that model's long-run mean. It is the composition used in the INFORMS 2026 study of interpretable allocation and model-predictive rebalancing (Varner, arXiv:2611.03022), built on the Jump-HMM of Alswaidan and Varner (arXiv:2603.10202).

What each piece is fit to

  • $\alpha_i$, $\beta_i$: an ordinary least squares single-index fit of the name's growth on the market's.
  • $f_s$: the equal-weight mean of the single-index residuals of the names in sector $s$, compounded into a price-like series and fit with its own Jump-HMM.
  • $\delta_i$: the leave-one-out slope of the name's residual on the mean of the other residuals in its sector, scaled once per sector so the generator reproduces the sector's mean pairwise residual covariance.
  • $s_i$: chosen so each name keeps its real variance; if the market and sector terms would explain more than 1 - floor of it, both loadings shrink and $s_i^2$ = floor. Names whose real R² is at least r2_threshold keep their real R² instead.

Why no copula

Names are linked only through the market and sector draws they share on the same day. A copula imposes a fitted dependence by reordering each name's draws across days, which would scramble the volatility clustering and the timing of jump episodes that the Jump-HMM produces. Here every name keeps its own day-by-day dynamics, and correlation across sectors comes only through the market term.

Fixed or drifting β

By default $\alpha_i$ and $\beta_i$ are fixed at their single-index estimates. Two options relax this:

  • β_scale (one value per name) makes β drift as an exponential moving average of normal shocks with half-life β_halflife trading days, started from its stationary distribution.
  • intercept_pull shrinks each $\alpha_i$ toward the universe mean (1 keeps the estimates, 0 sets every α to the mean).

Usage

using JumpHMM

tickers = ["SPY", "JPM", "BAC", "XOM", "CVX"]      # market first or anywhere; prices has one column per ticker
sectors = ["Financials", "Financials", "Energy", "Energy"]   # one label per non-market ticker, in order

portfolio = fit(PortfolioModel, tickers, prices;
                dependence=SectorFactorModel, market="SPY", sectors=sectors, N=50)
portfolio = tune(portfolio, prices)                 # tunes the market, sector, and name models
result = simulate(portfolio, 252; n_paths=100, seed=1)
result.results["SPY"]                              # the synthetic market paths are returned too

# options are forwarded with `sector_options`
drifting = fit(PortfolioModel, tickers, prices; dependence=SectorFactorModel, market="SPY",
               sectors=sectors, sector_options=(β_scale=fill(0.3, 4), β_halflife=126.0))

# one path of the whole universe, with the sector draws and the per-day β
d = sample_dependence(portfolio.dependence, 252; seed=1)

Every sector needs at least two names. The same seed reproduces a path exactly.

Calibrating the generator yourself

A caller that fits its own component models, or calibrates the drift against its own estimator, can use three tools (version 0.3.0):

  • fit(SectorFactorModel, ...; market_model, name_models, sector_models) stores models fitted or tuned elsewhere instead of fitting them; the noise centers come from the name models it stores.
  • sample_draws(model, n; seed) returns every random component of a path, and compose(model, draws) turns them into name growth rates. sample_dependence(model, n; seed) is the two together.
  • with_parameters(model; α, β_halflife, β_scale) copies the model with new intercepts and β drift.

Together they let a fit hold the randomness fixed and change only the parameters:

bank = [JumpHMM.sample_draws(model, n_days; seed=p) for p in 1:20]   # fixed draws
for k in (2.0, 4.0, 6.0)
    trial = JumpHMM.with_parameters(model; β_scale=fill(k, length(model.tickers)))
    G = [JumpHMM.compose(trial, d).G for d in bank]                # same draws, new drift
    # ... compare a statistic of G with its real value and keep the best k
end
