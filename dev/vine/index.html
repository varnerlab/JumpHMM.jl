Vine Copula

A vine copula builds the dependence of many assets from copulas of two assets, one on each edge of a sequence of trees. Each edge picks its own copula family. Different pairs of assets can then have different kinds of dependence.

The MyVineCopulaModel type holds a C-vine, in which each tree has one center asset linked to every later asset.

Bivariate copula families

Each edge holds one of five bivariate copula families:

TypeParametersTail dependence
MyGaussianBivariateCopulaModelthe correlation ρ, strictly between -1 and 1none
MyStudentTBivariateCopulaModelthe correlation ρ and the degrees of freedom ν, above 0both tails
MyClaytonBivariateCopulaModelthe dependence parameter θ, above 0lower tail
MyGumbelBivariateCopulaModelthe dependence parameter θ, at least 1upper tail
MyFrankBivariateCopulaModelthe dependence parameter θ, finitenone

Lower tail dependence means that two assets tend to fall together on their worst days. Upper tail dependence means that two assets tend to rise together on their best days. Each family can also be built from its parameters, for example JumpHMM.build(JumpHMM.MyClaytonBivariateCopulaModel, (θ = 2.0,)).

How build fits the vine

The build function fits the vine to a matrix of growth rates, with one row per day and one column per asset, in four steps:

  1. Uniform numbers: We replace each column of growth rates by its ranks divided by the number of days plus one.
  2. Order: We order the assets by the sum of the absolute Kendall's tau with every other asset, largest first.
  3. Tree $k$: The $k$-th asset in this order is the center of tree $k$, and it is paired with each later asset. We fit each of the five families to the pair from its Kendall's tau. The edge keeps the family with the smallest Akaike information criterion, two times the number of parameters minus two times the log-likelihood.
  4. Next tree: We replace the uniform numbers of each later asset by their conditional values given the center. Tree $k + 1$ then describes the dependence that is left after tree $k$.

The Student-t family also picks its degrees of freedom by log-likelihood. A family whose log-density is not finite on some day is skipped. A vine of $d$ assets has $d - 1$ trees, and tree $k$ has $d - k$ edges.

vine = JumpHMM.build(JumpHMM.MyVineCopulaModel, (growth_rates = R,));   # R: the growth rates (1/year), days × assets
vine.order                    # the order of the assets in the vine
vine.edges[1][1].copula       # the bivariate copula of the first edge of tree 1

Each edge is a MyVineEdgeModel with the two assets first and second, the assets it is conditioned on (conditioned), and its bivariate copula (copula).

Sample

The sample_dependence function draws uniform numbers from the vine, with one row per day and one column per asset. For each day, it draws one independent uniform number per asset and passes these uniform numbers through the inverse conditional distributions of the edges, from the last tree back to the first.

The draws come from the global random number generator. A portfolio uses the vine draws as it uses the uniform numbers of the other copulas.

U = JumpHMM.sample_dependence(vine, 252);   # 252 days × assets, every value strictly between 0 and 1
